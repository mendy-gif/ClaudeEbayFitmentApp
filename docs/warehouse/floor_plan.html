17,000 items will not all fit in this room at good access. The plan gives about 1,070 sq ft of rack footprint. My rough estimate is 7,000 to 9,000 items indoors, mostly small and medium parts in bins. Body panels, engines, wheels and slow movers need the outdoor option below, or a mezzanine.

Zones, right to left

  1. Drive lane, 28 × 16 ft inside the 11 ft door. Cars in, hulks out, forklift in and out over the ramp. Keep it clear; one waiting car max.
  2. Bay 1, lift, 14 × 26 ft, straight in from the door. 10,000 lb two-post asymmetric. Fluids drained on the lift as step one, drain cart rolls under.
  3. Bay 2, floor bay, 14 × 26 ft. Interior, lights, glass, body panels, modules. No lift needed for these. Anchors and 220 V run now so a lift drops in later.
  4. Teardown and utility, 28 × 16 ft behind the bays. Two benches, engine stand, six parts carts, waste oil and coolant tanks in the back corner, compressor beside them.
  5. Row E, bin shelving, 3 ft deep × 40 ft next to the bays. Fast small parts and this week's teardown output.
  6. Rows C and D, back-to-back pallet rack, 40 ft long. Medium and large parts on wire decks: headlights, radiators, control arms, seats, bumpers.
  7. Rows A and B, 32 ft long, behind the shipping pocket. Aisle 1 opens into packing, so put the fastest-selling stock here.
  8. Back walkway, 5 ft, with a 2 ft tire and wheel rack along the wall.
  9. Shipping and packing, 20 × 21 ft at the dock door. Pallet staging at the door, two 8 ft packing benches, label printer and scale.

Rack math: rows A (4 bays) + B (8) + C (10) + D (10) = 32 bays × 2 pallets × 4 levels = 256 pallet positions. Row E adds 40 ft of bin shelving, 7 shelves high. The 21 ft side of the roof takes one more beam level on whichever row sits under it.

Storage, two scenarios

  1. Indoors only. Add a steel mezzanine over rows C and D and the spine, roughly 2,000 sq ft at 9 ft, with bin shelving on both levels. Adds about 5,000 small items. Total about 13,000. Still short of 17,000, and the mezzanine costs roughly the same as four 40 ft containers.
  2. With outdoor storage (recommended). Keep small and medium fast movers inside. Put body panels, hoods, bumpers, engines, transmissions, wheels and anything slow outside in 40 ft high-cube containers on a gravel pad, each with door racks or pallet rack inside. One container holds roughly 60 doors upright or 20 pallets. Four containers cover the gap. A metal storage building later replaces the containers on the same pad.
  3. Either way, bins for every small part. Loose small parts on wire decks are the biggest waste of cube in a salvage warehouse.

Equipment to buy

  1. Two-post lift, 10,000 lb, asymmetric, 220 V single phase. Confirm slab first (core sample or landlord drawings, 4 in minimum, 3,000 psi).
  2. Electric reach stacker or walkie stacker, 16 ft lift, for the 9 ft rack aisles.
  3. Used counterbalance forklift, 5,000 lb, for the ramp, the yard, hulks and containers. Lives at the drive-in door.
  4. Pallet rack: 32 bays of 96 in beams, 42 in deep, 16 ft uprights, wire decking, column guards on aisle ends.
  5. Bin shelving 36 in × 18 in, 7 shelves, with bins. Row E and every mezzanine or container aisle.
  6. Drain cart, 250 gal waste oil tank, coolant tank, battery and airbag cabinets, 60 gal compressor with air drops at both bays.
  7. Six rolling parts carts, two teardown benches, two packing benches, scale, label printer.
  8. Tire rack, 2 ft deep, along the back wall.

Assumptions

  1. Door positions on the 93 ft wall: 10 ft dock door about 5 ft from the left corner, 3 ft man door then 11 ft drive-in door about 1 ft from the right corner.
  2. Office wing door opens somewhere on the right wall into the drive lane or bays. Nothing in the plan blocks that wall.
  3. Clear span, no columns, no doors on the back or left walls, slab at least 4 in.
  4. 10 people: 2 to 3 dismantling, 3 to 4 listing and photos in the office wing, 2 to 3 packing, 1 stocking and forklift.
  5. Item mix about 60% small, 30% medium, 10% large by count. The storage estimate moves with this.
