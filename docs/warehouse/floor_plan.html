Zones, front to back

  1. Drive lane, 58 × 14 ft. Cars enter through the 11 ft door on the right. Stripped shells leave the same way. Keep it empty; one waiting car max, on the right half only.
  2. Utility strip, 14 × 4 ft on the front wall between the doors. Waste oil and coolant tanks, air compressor, electrical panel if it lands here.
  3. Lift 1 and Lift 2, 14 × 26 ft each, right side, straight in from the 11 ft door. Two-post asymmetric lifts. Rolling tool carts and part bins live between the posts.
  4. Drain / prep bay, 14 × 26 ft. Ground level. Drain cart, battery and airbag cabinets, wheel dolly. Every car stops here before a lift. Slab and power are laid out so a third lift drops in with no rework.
  5. Packing, photo and shipping, 16 × 26 ft at the 10 ft door. Photo table with backdrop against the left wall, two packing benches, pallet staging by the door.
  6. Cross aisle, 58 × 10 ft. Forklift turns here. Forklift parking and charger in the right end by the office wall.
  7. Rack rows A to D, 40 ft long. A = left wall single row, small-parts bin shelving (sensors, modules, trim). B and C = back-to-back pallet rows for body panels, engines, transmissions. D = right wall single row, leave a gap wherever the office door is.
  8. Back walkway, 58 × 3 ft for egress and rack access from the far end.

Storage math: 6 rack faces × 5 bays × 2 pallets × 4 levels = 240 pallet positions. If Row A becomes bin shelving as suggested, that is 200 pallet positions plus 40 ft of bin shelving, 6 shelves high. The 21 ft side of the roof can take one more beam level on that wall's row.

Assumptions I made

  1. Both garage doors and the man door are on the 58 ft wall, in the order sketched: 10 ft door near the left corner, 3 ft door then 11 ft door near the right corner.
  2. Offices sit off the right 93 ft wall and have a door into the warehouse. Row D needs a gap there.
  3. No doors, columns or drains on the back wall or the left wall.
  4. You have or will get a forklift or walkie stacker with reach to 14 ft. Aisles are 12 ft for that reason. Hand-picking only would allow 8 ft aisles and one more double rack row.
  5. Slab is at least 4 in of sound concrete where the lifts go. Confirm before ordering lifts.
  6. Cars are pushed or driven in from the lot. A rollback does not need to back inside.
  7. Everything stays indoors. No yard for hulks, so the shell exits within a day of stripping.
