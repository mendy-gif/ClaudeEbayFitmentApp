Where the 17,000 items go. Main room: 80 drivetrain pallets, about 200 panels in slot bases, most medium parts on 205 ft of deep face, and about 4,500 fast small parts on 222 ft of shelving. Office wing: about 9,000 small parts plus light medium parts (lights, mirrors, trim) on roughly 700 ft of shelving, lifted up by forklift. Outside: wheels, slow panels, and drivetrain overflow in two to three containers along the left fence line, with the right brush cleared later for a metal building.

  2. Bay 1 lift and bay 2 floor bay, 14 × 24 ft each, straight in from the door.
  3. Forklift landing, 13 × 20 ft on the right wall behind the bays, in front of the second-floor opening. Assumed opening location, see assumptions.
  4. Teardown, 15 × 20 ft next to it: two benches, six carts, engine stand, oil and coolant in the corner.
  5. Back drivetrain aisle, 45 × 10.5 ft, forklift only, entered from the landing. Rack both sides, 4 levels at the 18 ft wall.
  6. Middle deep-rack block, 41 × 28.5 ft: one single and two double rows running left to right under the ridge, 5 faces × 41 ft. Bottom 6 ft slots for doors and hoods, beams every 18 in above for mechanical and medium. Cross aisle at the bay wall end for carts.
  7. Front spine, 45 × 12 ft, forklift and pick path. Put-away carts park here.
  8. Small-parts block, 20 × 37 ft behind shipping: 6 faces × 37 ft, aisles open into packing.
  9. Shipping and packing, 20 × 21 ft at the dock door.
  10. Office wing, upstairs: boxed small parts and light medium parts on 18 in shelving. Pallets go up by forklift. Downstairs: photo station, listing desks, office.

Drivetrain: 10 bays × 2 pallets × 4 levels = 80 single-deep pallets, against 49 engines + 65 transmissions today. Diffs, transfer cases and subframes go on the bottom level of the deep rack or outside.

How C differs from A and B

  1. Rows run left to right with the ridge, so every rack gets 18 to 21 ft instead of hitting the low wall at one end.
  2. Drivetrain gets a real single-deep rack on its own forklift aisle, the two-deep problem gone.
  3. Forklift inside is used for exactly three jobs: drivetrain row, second-floor opening, ramp. All other picking is by hand like today.
  4. Small parts mostly leave the main room for the office wing, which frees the floor for the heavy classes that cannot go upstairs.
  5. Shelf-face total is about 1,130 ft (205 deep + 222 small + ~700 office) against about 940 ft today, so 17k fits indoors with the office wing in play.

Upgrades that fit later

  1. Second lift in bay 2. Anchors and 220 V run on day one.
  2. Mezzanine over the deep-rack block at 9 ft, using the 21 ft ridge. Doubles the hand-pick face in the same footprint.
  3. Containers along the left fence after clearing the tree strip. Two to three 40 ft high-cubes for wheels, slow panels, drivetrain overflow.
  4. Metal storage building on the right side after clearing the brush. Replaces the containers.

Assumptions

  1. Second-floor opening is on the right wall, about 40 to 52 ft back from the front wall. If it is elsewhere, the landing and teardown swap places or move.
  2. Office wing upstairs floor takes shelving loads. Confirm before loading.
  3. Door positions on the 93 ft wall as sketched. Slab 4 in or better under the lift.
  4. Counterbalance forklift, 10.5 ft aisle. A 3-wheel electric fits in 9 ft and would widen the deep-rack block by 1.5 ft.

Equipment

  1. Reuse both lifts (one now), all pallet rack, all wire shelving, benches, carts, ladders.
  2. One forklift inside, 4,000 to 5,000 lb. The second stays outside or is sold.
  3. Drivetrain rack: 10 bays, 42 in deep, 16 ft uprights, 4 beam levels rated for engines on pallets.
  4. Dock board at the 10 ft door. Drain cart, waste oil tank, coolant tank, battery and airbag cabinets, compressor with drops at both bays.
  5. Pallet jack and engine hoist at the bays.
