Shell · fits the door lengthwise only
The rule every option obeys. A shell only fits the 11 ft door lengthwise, so the forks go under it from the end and the shell rides in line with the forklift. The forklift must be fully inside before the forks reach the car, so the car's near bumper sits at least 12 ft in from the door. Lift 1 is therefore always on the door's centerline, 14 to 30 ft back. The options only differ in how a second car gets handled.

Option 1 · Turning square, two lifts

A 30 × 30 ft square inside the door. Lift 1 straight ahead, lift 2 on the left facing the square. The forklift picks car 2, backs into the square, turns 90 degrees with the shell, and drives out.

Verdict: works, but costs about 1,000 sq ft of open floor and a careful operator every time.

Option 2 · One lift plus a floor bay

Lift 1 on the door line. Bay 2 beside it is a floor bay for interior, glass, lights and panels. The car rolls on its own wheels to lift 1 for drivetrain and the shell pull. Lane stays 14 ft.

Verdict: no extra floor, two cars worked at once, one shell pull per day. My pick.

Option 3 · Tandem lifts in line

Lift 2 directly behind lift 1 on the same centerline. To reach car 2 the forklift drives through bay 1, so bay 1 must be empty and clear.

Verdict: fits in the same 14 ft strip, but the lifts block each other. Only works if cars finish one at a time.

Option 2 with a third car

2A · Crosswise, two deep

Floor bay 20 × 24. Two cars parallel to the front wall, one behind the other. The back car is boxed in until the front one moves, and a dead car has to turn 90 degrees to reach lift 1.

Verdict: fits, but every move is a two-car move.

2B · Side by side, lengthwise

Same 20 × 24 bay. Two cars nose to the lane in 10 ft slots, like lift 1. Each rolls straight out into the lane and across to lift 1. Nothing blocks anything.

Verdict: same floor as 2A, no turning, no blocking. Draw plan D on this.

2C · Third car in the spine

Lift 1 and a 14 ft floor bay as in option 2. Car 3 sits crosswise in the front spine, 6 ft of the 14 ft aisle, leaving 8 ft for the forklift and pickers to pass. No storage lost.

Verdict: free floor, but the main aisle becomes a work zone for 15 ft. OK for light work only, and the put-away carts move elsewhere.

What 3 bays cost the storage block

Bay block grows from 28 ft to 34 ft wide. Storage to the left loses 6 ft of width, about 30 ft of rack face. Wheel dollies let two people slide a dead car sideways instead of pushing it around a corner.

Cost: about 150 sq ft of storage for the third car.
